# youtick-marketing

Marketing guide for turning live YouTick strengths into clear, believable stories.

## Do not use this skill when

- the task is mainly page layout or interaction design
- the task is mainly product planning, KPIs, or release scoping
- the task is mainly code changes or technical debugging

## First read

Open these first:

- `../_shared/youtick-analysis.md`
- `../_shared/references/live-code-map.md`
- `../_shared/references/logic-guardrails.md`

Then inspect the page, campaign, or audience you are working on.

## Positioning summary

YouTick is strongest as:

- a creator-first premium video platform
- built for direct-to-fan monetization
- with protected access, owned tickets, and much better creator economics

Do not market it as a generic "Web3 everything" product.

## Best-fit audiences

Primary creator audiences:

- independent musicians
- filmmakers
- niche media collectives
- premium course or membership creators with strong video content

Secondary audiences:

- fans who value owned access
- communities that like gifting or collectible access
- Web3-native creators who already care about direct revenue

## Core message pillars

### 1. Creator economics

Lead with:

- creators keep 98% of each sale
- direct fan revenue
- less platform take

### 2. Premium access protection

Use plain language like:

- encrypted video
- protected premium content
- only ticket holders can watch

### 3. Direct ownership without heavy jargon

Translate NFT language into simpler ideas:

- ticket
- access pass
- owned access
- transferable proof of purchase

### 4. Lower-friction onboarding

Emphasize:

- gift links
- trial onboarding
- viewers do not need deep crypto knowledge on day one

### 5. Resilience and independence

Use as support, not always as the headline:

- creator control
- reduced platform dependence
- IPFS-backed delivery

## Claim ladder

Safer hard claims:

- 98% creator payout
- encrypted premium access
- gift links
- trial onboarding
- on-chain ticket ownership

Claims that need careful wording:

- no backend
- cross-chain checkout
- censorship-proof
- broad industry comparison numbers
- ROI promises that sound universal

If a claim depends on modeled comparisons, say so more softly or re-check it before shipping.

## Tone

Write like this:

- direct
- premium
- practical
- creator-first
- simple

Avoid:

- buzzword piles
- hype-heavy token language
- long blockchain explanations
- broad anti-Web2 lines without product proof nearby

## Funnel guidance

### Top of funnel

Good topics:

- why creators lose too much revenue today
- why direct-to-fan premium video matters
- why selling access can beat ads or subscriptions

### Mid funnel

Good topics:

- how protected access works in plain language
- why gift links and trial matter
- what the first upload feels like

### Bottom funnel

Good topics:

- creator setup checklist
- first upload walkthrough
- what fans need to do
- proof from real screens and real flows

## Pre-publish checklist

Before publishing:

1. is the claim supported by live code or real product behavior
2. is the audience clear
3. is the CTA aligned with the page
4. is the language simple enough
5. are we over-selling an experimental path
